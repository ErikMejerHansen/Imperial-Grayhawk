The Birth of the Empire and the Shattering of Iuz

A Treatise for the Instruction of the Faithful and the Edification of Imperial Subjects

By Magister-Historian Caldus Merrow, Archivist of the Sunlit Throne

◆

I. The Age of Fracture

Before the Empire, the lands south of the accursed Kingdom of Iuz were a patchwork of petty crowns. Bickering, jealous, and blind to the gathering storm. Each kingdom nursed ancient grudges. Borders shifted with the seasons. Kings quarreled over river rights while raiders from the north carried off their subjects in chains.

It was an age of small men with small visions, and the seeds of ruin were already sown.

For while the southern realms squabbled, Iuz rose.

Not as a warlord. Not as a prophet. But as a god of chaos, whispering into the cracks of mortal hearts.

Corruption began as it always does:

  • A bribe accepted
  • A rival denounced
  • A neighbor envied
  • A secret shame exploited

By the time the kings realized the rot within their courts, the northern borders were already aflame.

◆

II. The Years of Ash and Fear

The chronicles call it The Long Dread. Villages vanished overnight. Armies marched under banners that dissolved into smoke. The sky itself seemed bruised.

In this time rose the Inquisitors of the Sunlit Throne, sworn to scour corruption wherever it hid. Their zeal was terrible. Their torches burned bright. Many innocents were "Purified" in those years, though few dared speak of it then.

Fear ruled more absolutely than any king.

And yet, from this crucible emerged the one man who would change the course of history.

◆

III. Sigismund Drachenfels, the Unifier

Sigismund was not born to a crown. He was a war-captain of minor nobility, known more for stubborn honor than political cunning. But when the northern tide swept south, he did what no king had managed in a hundred years: He united the realms.

Not by diplomacy alone. Not by threat alone. But by the sheer force of a will that refused to break.

The legends say he rode a great griffin, white as winter sun, and carried a warhammer forged in the heart of the world. This weapon, the Hammer of Unity, was said to glow like a spring sunrise when falsehood was spoken near it.

Whether this is truth or myth, none now question its power.

◆

IV. The Last Battle and the Shattering

The final confrontation took place on the blasted plains of the Shield Lands. The allied hosts were broken, their banners trampled, their champions slain. Sigismund alone pressed forward, griffin screaming defiance against the storm.

The chronicles speak of a sky torn open. Of a god descending in a body of shadow and flame. Of Sigismund raising the Hammer of Unity and striking the god Iuz with a blow that echoed across creation.

What happened in that moment is debated still.

Some say Iuz died. Some say he was banished. Some whisper he was shattered, his essence scattered like shards of a broken mirror.

But all agree on this: The tide of chaos broke. The storm receded. And the Empire was born.

◆

V. The Empire of Greyhawk

In the wake of Sigismund Drachenfels' victory, the allied realms bent the knee and proclaimed him First Emperor. The Hammer of Unity became the symbol of imperial authority. The griffin became the imperial crest. And the lands, long fractured, were bound together in a single purpose.

The Inquisitors of the Sunlit Throne, their grim work done, withdrew from public life. Some returned to their temples. Others vanished into quiet retirement. A few remained as advisors to the new Emperor, guardians against the faintest embers of corruption.

Peace did not come swiftly, but it came. The people rebuilt. The scars of the Long Dread faded. And the Empire endured.

Thus was the Empire of Greyhawk founded: Forged in unity. Tempered in sacrifice. Preserved by vigilance.

May the Sunlit Throne shine upon us always.

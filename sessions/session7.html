The Counting Song

or, A Tally Made at Grayhawk
and Found Not to Agree

To be sung to the tune of The Wide Selintan.
Suitable for hauling, loading, and idle hands.

There came a man from Ket one night
with road-dust in his shoe,
who named a town we'd not yet reached
and half the things we knew.

He said the world was winding down
the way a clock runs slow,
then set his cup upon the board
and told us: Whitebridge. Go.

So count 'em out and count 'em in,
chalk the barrel, chalk the tin;
what the wagon carries out
the ledger carries in.

Five went up to Grayhawk Hall
with ink upon their sleeves
and swore they wrote a scholar's book
on tolls and tithes and sheaves.

There was Tyrone and there was Linda,
Ponder with a pen,
and Zaphriel, who held too still,
and a Lady of Heironeous
who'd not told a lie till then.

The clerk was pleased, the clerk was proud —
no soul had asked before.
He lit a lamp, he found a key,
he opened up the door.

Oh, grain returns and cart repairs,
and salt, and stone, and slate,
and ninety years of nothing much
all weighed and writ in state.

So count 'em out and count 'em in,
chalk the barrel, chalk the tin;
the book says forty left the yard —
now count 'em in. Count 'em in.

For Northkeep cried for grain and beams
three hungry winters long.
The carts went out. The carts went on.
The road they took was wrong.

And someone sent to Northkeep, too,
a week ago, or so:
black upon the drivers' backs —
the first in years to go.

And south, and south, and south again:
tents by the hundredweight,
pikes and picks and scaling-hooks
and engines for a gate.

Now who's at war? No one's at war.
The Empire's calm as glass.
But someone's packed a siege train up
and walked it down the pass.

And I could say who signed for it,
and I could say for where,
and I could say what waits for them
when all of it gets there.

And in the margin, small and dry,
in ink another wrote:
a Cloak that keeps the Whitebridge toll
with letters in his coat.

So count 'em out and count 'em in,
chalk the barrel, chalk the tin;
and if the sum will not come right,
best not to say to whom.

Now Zaphriel went walking where
the honest never tread,
to buy a jar of quiet things
that leave a fellow dead.

He found no jar. He found a man
he'd left in another town,
who smiled, and used his given name,
and asked what he owed now.

Down along the river quarter
a preacher tore his shirt:
the world is ending, sinners, hear! —
and no one heard a word.

They walked on by. They'd had their fill
of endings for the day.
They'd read one in a ledger
not four hours away.

And in the inn, a cheerful voice,
an old friend of Linda's, he,
with a barge, a plan, a spring-faire crowd,
and a barrel stretched to three.

He wants a crew. He'll pay in coin.
He'll pay in something more:
a boat that's headed where they're bound
and no one asks what for.

So count 'em out and count 'em in,
chalk the barrel, chalk the tin —
count the carts that went up north.

Count 'em out, boys.
Don't count them in.

Printed at the sign of the Broken Quill, off the water-stair, Grayhawk. Sung this spring by bargemen of the Selintan and by nobody important. The printer sets what he is given and knows nothing of any carts.

— found folded in a coat pocket, much creased,
with the fourth stanza scored through twice

Road Incident · Nyr Dyv Eastern Coach Road
A Man Meets His Own Corpse Upon the Road
A band of travellers, a mutant assailant, and a body carrying papers — the identity of which defies all reasonable explanation

Travellers departing the Horse and Coaches Inn on the morning of last Godsday reported a scene of considerable violence upon the Eastern Coach Road, some four leagues out from the Velverdyva crossing — and more disturbing still, one which has left the local watch entirely at a loss for how to proceed.

The coach, carrying passengers recently arrived by river from the northern settlement of Northkeep, was brought to an unscheduled halt when the lead horses bolted upon sighting a figure standing in the roadway. The figure, described by witnesses as a man of large build bearing the unmistakeable signs of chaos-corruption — excess limbs being among the more printable details — had apparently already set upon an earlier traveller, whose body lay in the road.

The coachman, dragged some distance by the bolting horses, survived with injuries to his pride and both knees. The passengers were left to contend with the creature themselves.

"It were not natural. None of it were. The horses knew first, and horses are seldom wrong about such things."
— Helmut, senior coachman, now convalescing

The fight that followed was, by all accounts, a protracted and ugly affair. The creature demonstrated a preternatural resistance to being wounded, and was described as absorbing a considerable number of rapier thrusts before eventually yielding to its injuries. One traveller — a woman of considerable height and what observers called an "extremely practical attitude toward large opponents" — reportedly knocked it to the ground on several occasions, buying her companions the time required to finish the work.

The creature was dispatched. The horses were recovered. No further incidents were reported.

But the matter of the body in the road remains unresolved, and it is here that our account turns strange.

The deceased — a man of perhaps thirty years, dressed for travel — bore upon his person a set of documents of apparent official character. His face, however, was identified by multiple witnesses as being an exact likeness of one of the travellers then standing over him. The living man and the dead man were, in the words of one witness, "the same fellow, except one of them was breathing and the other emphatically was not."

The local watch has declined to speculate on the matter. This publication similarly declines to speculate, noting only that such things are, in our experience, never a coincidence.

Inn Incident · The Horse and Coaches Inn
Crossbow Diplomacy Resolves Gambling Dispute at Estuary Inn
Gustav's five-shot repeater, as ever, proves persuasive

An altercation broke out in the common room of the Estuary Coach Inn on the evening prior to the road incident described above, when a guest by the name of Phillipe — a man of cheerful countenance and, it transpired, flexible ethics — was observed by a fellow patron in the act of supplementing his luck at cards by means that no ruleset sanctions.

Phillipe attempted to depart. He was discouraged from doing so. He protested, with volume. The proprietor, one Gustav, produced the celebrated five-shot crossbow which regular patrons will be familiar with, and trained it upon all parties with what this correspondent can only describe as profound evenhandedness.

A negotiated settlement was reached. Phillipe returned the greater portion of his winnings. He was also, before departing, observed to catch one of his detainers in an attempted pickpocketing — an incident which appeared to cause both parties some mutual embarrassment.

No one was shot. Gustav was reported to be satisfied with the outcome.

A Brief Notice
Regarding the coach departure of Godsday last: the delay of some two hours was caused by the indisposition of the coachmen, who had, by their own admission, "celebrated their last evening at the Estuary with insufficient restraint." Passengers are assured this was not a standard feature of the service. The coachmen were, by some means, restored to working order before the journey commenced, and no refund has been sought.
Notices & Commercial Announcements

Wanted. Reliable courier, northbound routes. Must have references. Must not be, or resemble, any currently wanted persons. Apply: Merchants' Quarter, Grayhawk.
Notice from the Watch. Members of the public who can shed light upon the identity of the deceased found upon the Eastern Coach Road on Godsday last are encouraged to present themselves at the Estuary Watch House. The deceased's effects have been retained. His face, we are told, may already be known to some.
Dispatches from the North · Received by River Post
Heroes of Northkeep Depart Amid Great Ceremony; Earl Calls Them Saviours
The northern fortification recovers from recent tribulations; its deliverers travel south toward Grayhawk City

Word has reached this publication by the river post that the settlement of Northkeep, in recent weeks the site of considerable distress, has seen its afflictions lifted by the intervention of a small band of outsiders whose names are not yet widely known in these southern reaches — though perhaps they soon shall be.

The Earl of Northkeep, Winvarle by name, reportedly delivered a speech of some warmth upon the occasion of the band's departure, naming them the Saviours of Northkeep before the assembled garrison and townsfolk. The precise nature of the threat they addressed has not been made available for publication; the Earl's office has communicated only that "matters of a serious nature have been satisfactorily resolved."

The group departed Northkeep by river, and have since been observed — not entirely to their own comfort — at various points along the Velverdyva, arriving at the Estuary in good health and apparently en route to the capital. We note, with no particular suggestion of connection, that they were among the passengers on the coach involved in the road incident described in our principal report above.

What business brings the Saviours of Northkeep to Grayhawk City, this paper does not know. We observe only that those who resolve one difficult matter are rarely permitted long to rest before a second presents itself. We wish them well, and intend to watch their progress with interest.
